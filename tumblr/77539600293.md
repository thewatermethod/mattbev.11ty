My first exposure to the work of Ursula K Leguin was the SciFi Channel’s adaptation of this novel. Of which I made it through approximately 15 minutes of before I shut the television off. 

As a result, I had a badly misinformed view of Ms. LeGuin’s work. I am hardly qualified to speak to her output - at this point, she is easily a living legend. Offhand, I cannot think of a living science fiction author who has produced a body of work so influential and well-regarded.

Last year, I picked up “The Lathe of Heaven” and I absolutely loved it. I immediately read “The Dispossessed” and also loved it. I came across an old paperback of A Wizard of Earthsea and happily spent a dollar to pick it up.

I enjoyed this book, although less than I enjoyed the aforementioned other two books by her that I’ve read. I have to say, that was probably because this felt less political and generally more simple. I understand that this was the first book in a series, so I’m probably being unfair, but there it is. 

The lead character Ged, much like the protagonist in The Dispossessed, is complicated enough to seem unsympathetic at times, and it is a testament to LeGuin’s skill as a writer that she creates such vivid characterization with extreme economy of word and deed. 

You know…I’m not going to waste any more of my time telling you how great Ursula K LeGuin is.

Worth mentioning is that I was caught off guard to hear Ged described as having red-brown skin. It’s terrible that the SciFi channel made all the characters white, considering how whitewashed the genre is. Read the book to make up for that obnoxious act.
