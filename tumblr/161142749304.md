> > > 
> > > _On the 23rd of August 1914, five Russian Armies marched southeast into the Empire of Austria-Hungary. In front of them lay the vast fertile plains of Poland and Ukraine, the treacherous mountain passes of the Carpathians, an army that spoke seven languages, and Bela Lugosi._
> > > 
> > > Be'la Ferenc Dezso Blasko was born on October 20, 1882, in Lugos, Austria-Hungary (now [Lugoj](http://www.britannica.com/place/Lugoj), Romania), to Paula de Vojnich and István Blaskó, a banker. He was the youngest of four children, and rather than follow in the family business, he ran away from home at the age of eleven where he worked odd jobs and found his true calling –acting.
> > > 
> > > On June 28th, 1914 Austrian-Hungarian Archduke Franz Ferdinand and his wife Sophie were shot to death by a member of the “Black Hand”: a Serbian separatist group with ties to Russia. The Archduke’s grieving father, Emperor Franz Joseph, ordered a draconian crackdown on Serbia, which quickly declared independence and announced an alliance with Czarist Russia. Kaiser Wilhelm of Germany came to the Emperor’s aid. France came to Russia’s aid. Britain came to France’s aid. Over a million Russian troops marched on Austro-Hungary. One and a quarter million German troops marched through Belgium and into France. Turkey joined Austro-Hungary and Germany. Seeking to regain lost territory, formerly neutral Italy and Romania attacked Austro-Hungary. Bulgaria declared war on Romania. Eventually the U.S. would get involved. The First World War (1914-1918) was on.  
> > > 
> > > All of this was beyond the horizons of the small town of Lugos. Even though he could have been exempted from service, Be’la saw his nation as the victims of Serbian terrorism and Russian invasion. As an ethnic Hungarian he volunteered, enlisting in the 43rd Royal Honvèd\* Infantry and was commissioned a Hadnagy (Lieutenant) in the summer of 1914.
> > > 
> > > ![image](https://thewatermethod.com/tumblr/thewatermethod.tumblr.com/media/f5e253ffa667e48528dd67d54c306f77d6897af381b9b867560bc1c64c3eb0bf.jpg)
> > > 
> > > > _Source: Budapest’s [hadtörténeti múzeum](http://www.militaria.hu/hadtorteneti-intezet-es-muzeum)_  
> > > 
> > > Some biographers say he “_served in the trenches for two years_” while this is a convenient short synopsis, it conceals a tragic truth. For the first year and a half on the Eastern Front, generals deployed their troops as their grandfathers and ancestors had done. Strategists on both sides were convinced that early aggressive attacks would result in a short war. Both sides were wrong. Cavalry charged on horseback, Infantry attacked in massed columns and artillery was pushed forward to the front line. None of these tactics were viable in the age of the machine gun and the light mortar. Casualties were horrific. Fifty percent losses became the norm.
> > > 
> > > In a series of massive attacks and counter attacks between August 23rd and September 11th 1914, which historians  refer to as the [Great Battle of Galicia](http://www.consimgames.com/docs/Golovin%20Battle%20of%20Galacia%20article.pdf), Russia sent 38½ Infantry Divisions and 17 ½ Cavalry Divisions southwest toward Lemberg, hoping to route the Austro-Hungarians before winter set in.  
> > > 
> > > Russian spies had informed the generals that the Empire’s plan was to deploy their troops in defensive positions on the south bank of the Dneister. The spies had been misled. The Austro-Hungarians attacked the Russian north flank from the cover of the Tanew woods with 19 ½ Infantry Divisions and 4 Cavalry Divisions, decimating the Russian 4th Army in the battle of Lublin. The Empire celebrated a great victory –too soon.
> > > 
> > > Few nations in the history of warfare would take the loss of six divisions and keep moving forward. Unfortunately for the Empire, Russia was one of them. The Russian Ninth Army arrived with 10 fresh divisions and the force renewed the advance. The armies of the Empire were forced back behind the San River at the foot of the Carpathians. This was not going to be a short war.
> > > 
> > > During this campaign, Lt. Blasko received the first and most serious of three wounds near the town of [Rohatin](http://www.personal.ceu.hu/students/97/Roman_Zakharii/rohatyn.htm) (Rohatyn) Galicia in modern-day western Ukraine. He was awarded a wound medal equivalent to the U.S. Purple Heart and returned to active duty.   
> > > 
> > > Like many veterans, especially those from armies that fought against his adopted country, he seldom spoke of the war in later years.  He once had to explain to a director that corpses are heavy and hard to move – he knew this because he once had to hide under them to avoid capture. He also told this amazing story:
> > > 
> > > > _“We were protecting a forest from the Russians. All of us were cowering beneath huge trees, each man beneath a tree. A young officer incautiously, went a little way out of cover and a bullet struck his breast. I forgot the Russians were firing from their line with machine guns. Not a selfless man…I ran to him and gave him first aid. I came back to my tree and found out that it had been blown to the heavens in heavy crushing pieces. I became hysterical. I wept there on the forest floor, like a child…not from fear, not even from relief…from gratitude at how God had paid me back for having that good heart.”_
> > > 
> > > As the war progressed, Bel’a rose to the rank of Százados (Captain) of elite ski troops fighting in the Carpathians: the same mountain ranges that had halted the invasions of Turks, Mongols and Romans in past centuries.  
> > > 
> > > In a war infamous for brutality, the Carpathian campaign [stands out](http://www.historynet.com/carpathian-catastrophe.htm). Not only were combat casualties as high as on any active front, the combination of poor supply, inadequate medical care, rampant disease and cold contributed to many regiments on both sides simply disintegrating. When it did arrive, food was frequently frozen solid.

> > > 
> > > The Carpathians were attacked three times by Russians and later Romanians between 1914 and 1916 but were never taken. Captain Blasko was wounded for the final time [five miles from the ruins of Castle Voivode](http://www.militarian.com/threads/bela-lugosi-is-dracula.7704/). Not discovered until 1972, [Voivode](http://nightiesofthelivingdead.tumblr.com/post/124423013073) was the mountain fortress built to resist the Turks by Vlad Dracula.  
> > > 
> > > A month after his discharge in January 1916, Captain Bel’a Blasko was performing in Budapest’s National Theater under a stage name derived from the name of his home town –Bela Lugosi.  
> > > 
> > > ![image](https://thewatermethod.com/tumblr/thewatermethod.tumblr.com/media/ebfa6543fb10e1dbc7e4206dfda3a930ba01f06a666783b6ac6475fec3bdad06.jpg)
> > > 
> > > In the following years Bela found himself in many dramatic scenes: on the wrong side of a revolution, on Mihael Horthy’s death list, crossing the border with his wife hidden under hay bales, working as a crewman on freighter bound for New Orleans, an illegal immigrant in New York, and finally an actor again on stage and screen, but these are stories for another time.

> > > > _\*For practical communication purposes, The Austro-Hungarian Empire required that all officers spoke either German or Hungarian and assigned them to units accordingly. Some sources identify the_ [Honvèd](https://europebetweeneastandwest.wordpress.com/tag/honved/) _as the Hungarian National Guard [reserves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Hungarian_Honv%C3%A9d). This is untrue, as Honvèd troops were organized into divisions, equipped with artillery and trained as front line combatants. The 23rd. Honved Division, for example, were the principal defenders of the fortress of Przemyśl, the Verdun of the eastern front, and the fictionalized back story for the Bela Lugosi/Boris Karloff film “The Black Cat” (1934)._
