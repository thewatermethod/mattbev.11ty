> > > > I love _Max Headroom_ because the way they achieved the title character’s signature look could only have come from that particular point in time. It was essential for Max to appear computer-generated (being an AI and all), and computer graphics were _just_ becoming common enough that everybody knew what shitty CGI looked like, but even shitty CGI was still too expensive to use in a TV show, so the guy playing Max put on rubber facial prostheses to make himself look low-poly, and trained himself to move like he was poorly animated. They _used practical effects to fake bad CGI_ – what could be more beautiful?

> > I _think_ the animated background behind him was actual computer graphics, although it looks less pixellated than we could generate on home computers.
> > 
> > But I’m not 100% sure it’s not hand-drawn￼. A lot of what looks like early cgi on 20th century TV shows right up into the 90s is traditional hand/cell or even stop motion animation, so sometimes it’s hard to tell. (See for example all the touchscreens on _Star Trek: TNG;_ none of that was possible then.)
> > 
> > The “crappy cgi” Frewer’s makeup job was imitating was insanely cool cgi that cost millions of dollars at the time. And the graphics glitches are down to video editing; you noticed that some of them or more akin to tape tracking/magnetic tape damage because at the time we’d seen more videotape glitches than computer graphics.
