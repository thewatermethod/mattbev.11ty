> Today marks the 100th anniversary of the assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand — the event that triggered World War I and reshaped the world as we know it.
> 
> World War I produced a host of poets and authors who recorded its horrors in memorable words — if you haven’t read Vera Brittain’s [T](http://www.theguardian.com/books/2013/mar/24/vera-brittain-testament-of-youth)[estament of Youth](http://www.theguardian.com/books/2013/mar/24/vera-brittain-testament-of-youth) yet, go get yourself a copy right now.
> 
> The poem that has always stuck with me, though, is Wilfred Owen’s ”[Dulce et Decorum Est](http://www.english.emory.edu/LostPoets/Dulce.html)”
