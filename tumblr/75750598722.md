I remember fighting to stay away all the way through that Caveman movie from the mid 1980’s as a child (I’m sure I could find the title with thirty seconds worth of google searching, but w/e… something about fire.) Between that and the interminable first chapter of Alan Moore’s Voice of the Fire, I’ve generally found my realistic prehistoric man stories to be pretty unexciting. They need to be Conan flavored for me to really enjoy them.

![](https://thewatermethod.com/tumblr/thewatermethod.tumblr.com/media/b4a5545dc964f1df165e8a5d5c64399699e35963100708f8b943e2044cb3f568.jpg)

That said, [Kim Stanley Robinson’s Shaman](http://www.amazon.com/Shaman-Kim-Stanley-Robinson/dp/0316098078/ref=sr_1_1?ie=UTF8&qid=1391650375&sr=8-1&keywords=shaman "The Book on Amazon") is a fairly enjoyable realistic Caveman story. It bucks a lifelong trend and manages to be reasonably paced and interesting to read, and, notably, the entire book is in readable English. 

I must admit that I bought it primarily on the strength of a dimly remembered good review (likely on Boing Boing) and the title, which led me to make some wrong assumptions about the content of the book.

The book concerns the struggle for survival and the coming of age of a boy named Loon, apprenticed to a shaman named Thorn. There is an excellent moment at the end of the initial chapters where, after witnessing his terrifying adolescent initiation rite, we find out Loon’s age. (No spoilers, but he’s younger than you imagine he is, at first.)

Loon doesn’t want to be a shaman, and this is one of the central struggles of the book. The other, and always more pressing struggle, is the struggle to stay alive. 

Both struggles are given equal weight, if not equal time, and both are resolved satisfactorily. Robinson’s prose is brisk and workmanlike (a high compliment), and as I said before, he doesn’t fall into the trap of invented words or what have you that tends to bog this sort of story down.

Reading this book you will, at the very least, you will gain a strong appreciation for your ability to go the grocery store and find food waiting for you, but likely you will gain a great deal more.
