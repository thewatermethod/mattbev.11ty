I had read bits and pieces of this. Growing up, the comic store had bits and pieces of it for sale and I would grab an issue or two when I had some extra candy money. A few years ago, i grabbed the first trade of Swamp Thing, mainly to reread the famous initial story “Anatomy Lesson.” A great story mainly for the clever central conceit rather than the execution.

It was strange, to re-read these stories. Alan Moore’s impact on comics, much of it through these stories, cannot be overstated. But compared to his later work, this work is noticeably the product of a less experienced writer. Generally, there is one strong, clever idea on which a story hangs, but the story seldom does anything that really lives up to Moore’s reputation. He hadn’t yet come into his **formal** mastery, that is, although his scripts were probably as fabulously detailed as they remain today, he was not creating these incredibly whole pieces like, for example, some sections of Promethea or From Hell.

In Moore’s later career, every aspect of the comic has been so completely considered that artistically and thematically the comic is tightly constructed. These comics feel a lot more like comics. I can’t really speak to why.

The art in these collection is where the comic really shines. Bissette’s art veers from psychedelic to horrifying and he pulls each panel and each page off masterly.

The stories are grimy, EC comics/Universal horror pastiche. The story that introduces the character of John Constantine comes with one of the moments I found most unsettling. They just don’t rise to the level of _From Hell._
