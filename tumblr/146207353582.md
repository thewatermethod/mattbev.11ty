> **Doctor Who rewatch: _Unearthly Child_**  
> 
> Ten things I love: 
> 
> 1.  The amazing Twlight Zone style intro with the whooshy white swirls, “DOCTOR OHO” and Delia’s Derbyshire’s surreal arrangement
> 2.  The unearthly hum of the TARDIS, the first character we ever see
> 3.  Barbara Wright is the first regular we “meet” in all of Doctor Who besides the TARDIS, no-nonsense space mom
> 4.  Ian and Barbara’s easygoing rapport and friendship_,_ banter and intelligence; they’re kindred spirits from scene one
> 5.  Susan’s “unearthliness” — that combo of genius and otherworldly, yet so fond of the 20th century and an ordinary teen
> 6.  The original reason Ian and Barbara got involved was because Barbara was concerned about child abuse. For Ian it was curiosity and respecting Barbara’s instincts, but Babs really did want to make sure the Doctor wasn’t abusing his granddaughter, which is a rather modern motivation for a companion to get swept up in his adventures!
> 7.  Ian and Barbara’s hands on the TARDIS door, feeling the faint vibration like a heartbeat, as they realize “it’s alive!”
> 8.  Barbara bursting into the TARDIS to rescue Susan, and through her eyes we see our first unforgettable glimpse of the original console room. The moment when we, through the audience POV character, step over the threshold into the world of Doctor Who.
> 9.  The argument between the adults all being _so very much themselves_ and angry and concerned and imperfect, with Susan trying so hard to mediate because she cares about all of them.
> 10.  The TARDIS materialising in a new time with the shadow and “Dr. Who” in the credits (SO THERE)
> 
> Really, the first episode lives up to its title, and it’s got a timeless, eerie quality of leaving the real world for a darker, scarier one that’s held up over 50 years. Apart from the “red indian” comment and the uncharacteristically elaborate/scary dematerialise/materialise sequence for the TARDIS’ first on-screen journey— apparently it took the Doctor some time to learn to operate it correctly— it’s astoundingly flawless.
