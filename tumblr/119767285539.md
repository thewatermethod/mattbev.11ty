> 
> So, when I was a kid I liked to draw a lot. My mom was horrified one day to find a sketch of a pink, pulpy, upside-down monster with a frowny face with a speech bubble pleading, “Help me!” My mom was concerned, but she didn’t understand. I was in love with Brundlefly.
> 
> It wasn’t until much later that I saw the original Fly, which, if you’ll excuse me for saying, is far less dreamy than the Goldblum equivalent. Both movies feature scientists too ambitious for their own good–a downfall of many monster movie affairs. Whereas the 1958 film takes a very literal approach on the make-up, Cronenberg, king of body horror, presents audiences with a disgustingly feasible representation of what a human-fly hybrid might look like. 
> 
> Spoiler: It’s gross.
> 
> But, in both cases, the humanity of the mind behind the monster breaks through. I’m going to assume that’s what I picked up on as a child. Or maybe it’s that Jeff Goldblum is irresistible regardless of his state of decomposition. Who knows.
